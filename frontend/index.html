The front door

Nineteen live things,
one front page.

The lab stopped hiding its inventory behind a menu. This pass prints the whole shelf — every tool, every room, every small utility — with its address.

Everything in this paper is running. The audio lab slices an uploaded track into beats and rebuilds it as a loop, a canon, or a phase-shifted layer. The rooms hold a streaming client, a terminal relay, and a profile builder that never grew up. The tool shelf is a row of small services that each do one job. The back page keeps the voxel engine, the sandbox, a map of Edmonton, and a phone app for keeping the internet out.

None of it is a mock-up and none of it says coming soon. Nineteen entries, four departments, and one sign-in in front of the parts that need it. Five landing runs have been made for this address; the ones that came before are kept whole at the archive, screenshots and all.

The paper itself is the front door this time. Read the leads, then use the classifieds at the bottom of the page — every line is a working link.
